# Competitive Analysis Framework

Evaluation dimensions used by the `competitive-analysis` skill.
Apply these consistently across all competitors so the output is directly comparable.

---

## 1. Positioning

**What to assess:** How does the competitor frame their product in the market? Who is their stated target customer? What problem do they claim to solve, and how do they differentiate?

**Evidence to look for:**
- Homepage headline and value proposition
- "Who it's for" messaging (SMB, enterprise, developer, consumer, etc.)
- Categories they use to describe themselves (e.g., "AI writing assistant", "workflow automation")
- Taglines, press releases, and analyst quotes

**Key question:** Where do they compete — and who are *they* trying to beat?

---

## 2. Pricing

**What to assess:** Pricing model, tiers, and positioning (budget / mid-market / premium). What the free tier (if any) includes. What gates expensive tiers.

**Evidence to look for:**
- Pricing page structure and tier names
- What each tier unlocks (seats, features, usage limits)
- Whether pricing is public or sales-gated (sales-gated = enterprise signal)
- Annual vs monthly options and discount size

**Key question:** At what price point do they compete, and what is the monetization lever (seats, usage, features)?

---

## 3. Features

**What to assess:** Core capabilities, differentiating features, and notable gaps. Focus on features that matter to the target customer segment, not exhaustive feature lists.

**Evidence to look for:**
- Feature pages and comparison tables
- Changelog and release notes (what they're investing in)
- Integration ecosystem (what they connect to)
- User reviews mentioning specific features

**Key question:** What do they do better than anyone else, and what is conspicuously absent?

---

## 4. Weaknesses

**What to assess:** Known pain points, common complaints, and structural limitations. Distinguish between execution weaknesses (bugs, slow support) and structural ones (architecture, business model).

**Evidence to look for:**
- Review sites (G2, Capterra, Reddit, Hacker News)
- Churned customer commentary
- Features competitors advertise as advantages over this product
- Support forums and public issue trackers

**Key question:** Where do customers leave, and why?

---

## 5. Moat

**What to assess:** What makes this competitor hard to displace? Network effects, data advantages, switching costs, brand, regulatory position, ecosystem lock-in.

**Evidence to look for:**
- Integration depth (how embedded are they in the customer's workflow?)
- Data network effects (does the product get better with more users/data?)
- Switching cost signals (migration complexity, proprietary formats, contractual lock-in)
- Brand strength and category ownership

**Key question:** If a new entrant built the same features, what would stop customers from switching?

---

## Cross-competitor patterns to note

After analyzing individual competitors, look for:
- **Pricing clusters** — do competitors price in the same ranges, or is there a gap?
- **Positioning overlap** — are multiple players saying the same thing? That's a market signal.
- **Shared weaknesses** — if everyone has the same complaint, there's a white space
- **Feature convergence** — if everyone has a feature, it's table stakes; if nobody does, it's either a gap or a deliberate choice
